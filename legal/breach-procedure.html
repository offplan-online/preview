Data Breach Response Procedure

Legal basis: GDPR Art. 33–34; AU NDB Scheme; UAE PDPL Owner: [DPO / CTO / Legal] Version: 0.1 (draft) Test frequency: Annually (tabletop exercise)

This procedure defines how offplan.online responds to personal data breaches. GDPR Art. 33 requires notification to the supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of a breach likely to result in risk to individuals. This procedure ensures we meet that obligation.

1.What Counts as a Data Breach?

A personal data breach is any security incident leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to personal data. Examples:

  • Database exposed publicly (misconfigured S3 bucket, leaked credentials)
  • Ransomware encrypting customer data
  • Email sent to wrong recipient containing personal data
  • Unauthorised internal access (employee accessing data without authorisation)
  • Sub-processor breach affecting our data
  • Physical loss of a device containing unencrypted personal data
  • Account takeover of a customer admin account exposing their end-user data

Not a breach (but log anyway): attempted but unsuccessful attack; accidental access immediately reversed with no data extracted; loss of non-personal data.

2.Severity Classification

Severity Criteria Examples Response Time
Critical Mass exposure of personal data; ransomware; complete system compromise; sensitive data (financial, health) involved Database dump publicly accessible; ransomware on production servers Immediate — all-hands
High Significant number of data subjects affected (>100); data likely exfiltrated; real risk of harm API key exposed, data accessed; credential stuffing resulting in account takeovers Within 2 hours
Medium Limited exposure; small number of data subjects; low risk of harm; data not exfiltrated Email sent to wrong customer; single account compromised with no sensitive data Within 8 hours (business)
Low No personal data exposed; or accidental access immediately corrected; no risk of harm Internal system error logs briefly accessible; test data exposed Log and monitor

3.Response Timeline (72-Hour Clock)

Hour 0

Detection & Immediate Containment

  • Incident detected (automated alert, customer report, sub-processor notification, internal discovery)
  • Immediately notify Engineering lead and DPO/Legal — do not wait to investigate first
  • Start the Incident Log (date/time first detected, who detected, initial scope)
  • Immediate containment: revoke exposed credentials, take affected system offline if necessary, block attacker IP
  • Do NOT destroy evidence — preserve logs
Clock starts now 72 hours begins at the moment we become "aware" of a likely breach — not when investigation is complete.
0 – 4h

Initial Assessment

  • Classify severity (Critical / High / Medium / Low — see §2)
  • Identify: what data was affected, how many data subjects, categories of data (special categories?)
  • Determine if this is a controller breach (our data) or processor breach (customer's end-user data)
  • For processor breaches: notify affected Customer(s) without undue delay (target: within 4 hours) per DPA §7
  • Engage external legal counsel if Critical or High
  • Engage cyber insurer if Critical
  • Notify CEO
4 – 24h

Investigation & Risk Assessment

  • Full forensic investigation: what data was accessed/exfiltrated, by whom, how
  • Determine whether notification to supervisory authority is required:
    • Notification is required if breach is "likely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons"
    • No notification needed if breach is "unlikely to result in a risk" — document this conclusion
  • Determine whether notification to data subjects is required (Art. 34 — "high risk")
  • Draft Art. 33 notification to Cyprus DPA (use template in §5)
  • Implement further containment and remediation measures
24 – 72h

Supervisory Authority Notification

  • Submit Art. 33 notification to Cyprus DPA before the 72-hour deadline
  • If all information not yet available, submit what is known and state investigation is ongoing (Art. 33(4) allows phased notification)
  • If AU data subjects affected: notify OAIC under Notifiable Data Breach scheme
  • If UAE data subjects affected: assess UAE PDPL notification obligation
  • Continue remediation: patch, rotate credentials, improve monitoring
  • Update affected Customers with full details if processor breach
Safe harbour Documenting that a breach was "unlikely to result in a risk" and keeping that record is your protection if challenged later. Always document the reasoning, even for low-severity events.
72h –
7 days

Data Subject Notification (if required)

  • If "high risk" to data subjects (Art. 34): notify affected individuals directly, without undue delay
  • Notification must include: nature of breach, DPO contact, likely consequences, measures taken
  • For Customer's End Users: Customer (controller) is responsible for Art. 34 notification; we provide them the information they need
  • Maintain communication with DPA — respond to any follow-up questions
5–30
days

Post-Incident Review

  • Root cause analysis: how did the breach occur?
  • Remediation plan: what changes prevent recurrence?
  • Update security controls, access policies, monitoring rules
  • Publish Post-Incident Report (PIR) internally; share summary with affected Customers
  • Complete Incident Log entry and file in breach register (§6)
  • Review and update this procedure if gaps identified
  • Consider notifying cyber insurer of potential claim

5.Art. 33 Notification Template

Submit via Cyprus DPA online portal at dataprotection.gov.cy. Required information (GDPR Art. 33(3)):

Mandatory Content
  1. Nature of the breach — type (confidentiality, integrity, availability breach), categories and approximate number of data subjects concerned, categories and approximate number of records concerned
  2. Contact details — name and contact details of DPO or other point of contact
  3. Likely consequences — the likely consequences of the breach
  4. Measures taken — measures taken or proposed to address the breach, including measures to mitigate its possible adverse effects

If all information is not yet available when submitting, state this clearly and provide further information as soon as possible (Art. 33(4) allows phased notification).

7.Australian NDB Requirements

If the breach affects Australian data subjects and offplan.online has AU customers, the Notifiable Data Breach (NDB) scheme under the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) applies:

  • Assess within 30 days of suspecting an "eligible data breach" (serious harm likely)
  • Notify OAIC and affected individuals as soon as practicable
  • Notification form: oaic.gov.au — Notify us of a data breach
  • Different threshold to GDPR (focus on "serious harm" not just "risk") — may require separate assessment

8.Annual Test

This procedure must be tested annually via a tabletop exercise. The exercise simulates a breach scenario, walks the team through this procedure, and identifies gaps. Results are documented and used to update the procedure. Next scheduled test: [DATE].

9.Incident Response Checklist (print and use during an incident)

  • Hour 0: Incident detected — start clock, notify Engineering lead & DPO immediately
  • Hour 0: Start Incident Log — date, time, who detected, initial description
  • Hour 0: Immediate containment — revoke credentials, isolate systems if needed
  • Hour 0: Preserve evidence — do not delete logs
  • Hour 0–2: Classify severity (Critical / High / Medium / Low)
  • Hour 0–4: Notify CEO
  • Hour 0–4: Engage external counsel if Critical or High
  • Hour 0–4: Notify affected Customers if processor breach
  • Hour 4–24: Full investigation — scope, data affected, number of data subjects
  • Hour 4–24: Decide: notification required? Document reasoning either way
  • Hour 4–24: Draft Art. 33 notification (use template in §5)
  • Hour 24–72: Submit Art. 33 to Cyprus DPA
  • Hour 24–72: Submit NDB notification to OAIC if AU data subjects affected
  • 72h+: Art. 34 data subject notification if "high risk" — or document why not required
  • 5–30 days: Root cause analysis and remediation plan
  • 5–30 days: Post-Incident Report (PIR) — internal + Customer summary
  • Complete: Update Breach Register entry and close incident
